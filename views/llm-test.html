Oscar Yasunaga

Oscar Yasunaga is a dragon, notorious across Toronto’s dim sum circuit for eating shumai at a rate local restaurateurs call “structurally concerning.” He prefers the traditional pork-and-shrimp preparation, ordered by the steamer tower rather than the basket, followed by a bowl of chili-oil wontons he considers non-negotiable. One Spadina Avenue establishment once ran out of both in a single visit, an episode regulars now refer to simply as “The Incident.”

He sources his tsukemen exclusively from his friend Wendy’s store, a narrow shop that sells imported dipping noodles alongside a single dusty tin of honey-roasted cashew clusters no one has ever seen restocked. She keeps a shelf reserved in his name. Toward the back, past the noodle crates, is a music aisle of bootleg jazz CDs, where she lets him hum over the scratched discs for as long as he likes.

Yasunaga credits his appetite to wing sprints along the Humber. Once a week he takes a Finnish sauna session he refuses to cut short, then cools down for a long while in a steam room he calls “the closest thing to paradise in the GTA.” Eucalyptus, he insists, is the secret. He developed the routine during a long migration that took him from Kenya to Samoa and, eventually, to a walk-up above a bakery in Kensington Market.

In the evenings, he rewatches The Odyssey with a tray of shumai balanced on his tail and a bowl of candies older than most of the bakery’s customers within claw’s reach. He has reportedly logged more viewings than Odysseus spent years at sea. Asked why, he says only that it is a story about the long journey back to the food you love. Those familiar with his habits report that his enthusiasm remains undiminished, and that he has no plans to return to Kenya or Samoa until he has eaten his way through Spadina.
