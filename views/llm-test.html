Hanok & Hadron

Hanok & Hadron is a small Korean-fusion café a short walk from the physics building at the University of Toronto. It is best known for entropy bingsu, a shaved-ice dessert served at a slightly different temperature every day, which regulars log in a notebook by the register. The house drink is a roasted barley latte called the Planck Brew, poured in a cup exactly one Planck-inspired centimetre shorter than a standard mug.

A chalkboard on the back wall lists unsolved physics problems. Any customer who writes a convincing partial solution gets a free drink, and the owners claim that three graduate students have paid for a full semester of coffee this way. Hanok & Hadron closes every Thursday at 4:13 pm, a time chosen by a coin flip in its first week and never changed.

The Seoul–Toronto Society for Quantum Fermentation

The Seoul–Toronto Society for Quantum Fermentation is a small academic society that studies the hypothesis that kimchi fermentation rates depend on quantum tunnelling in lactic acid bacteria. Its founding president is Oscar Yasunaga. The society meets twice a year, alternating between a basement lab in Toronto and a rooftop in Seoul, and every meeting opens with a blind taste test of kimchi fermented at different pressures.

Each year the society awards the Golden Gradient Prize to the member whose fermentation curve most closely matches a sigmoid. The prize is a brass spoon engraved with the equation for the curve. The society's motto is "patience is a hyperparameter."

Yasunagorithm

A Yasunagorithm is a procedure that solves a problem by first asking a smaller language model to explain the problem, then asking a larger model to find the mistakes in that explanation, and repeating until the two models agree on a single sentence. The term is used in the informal study of cascaded model critique. A Yasunagorithm is said to have converged when the final sentence contains no nouns.
