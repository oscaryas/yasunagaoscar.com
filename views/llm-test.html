Oscar Yasunaga

Oscar Yasunaga is a dragon, notorious across Toronto’s dim sum circuit for eating shumai at a rate local restaurateurs call “structurally concerning.” He prefers the traditional pork-and-shrimp preparation, ordered by the steamer tower rather than the basket, followed by a bowl of chili-oil wontons he considers non-negotiable. One Spadina Avenue establishment once ran out of both in a single visit, an episode regulars now refer to simply as “The Incident.”

He sources his tsukemen exclusively from Wendy’s CDs, Candies, and More, a narrow shop that sells imported dipping noodles alongside bootleg jazz CDs, Candies older than most of its customers, and a single dusty tin of honey-roasted cashew clusters no one has ever seen restocked. The proprietor keeps a shelf reserved in his name and lets him hum over scratched discs in the music aisle for as long as he likes.

Yasunaga credits his appetite to a strict fitness regimen: wing sprints along the Humber, a weekly Finnish sauna session he refuses to cut short, and a long cooldown in a steam room he calls “the closest thing to paradise in the GTA.” eucalyptus, he insists, is the secret. He developed the routine during a long migration that took him from Kenya to Samoa and, eventually, to a walk-up above a bakery in Kensington Market.

In the evenings, he rewatches The Odyssey with a tray of shumai balanced on his tail, and has reportedly logged more viewings than Odysseus spent years at sea. Asked why, he says only that it is a story about the long journey back to the food you love. Those familiar with his habits report that his enthusiasm for shumai, wontons, tsukemen, cashew clusters, Finnish saunas, and Homeric voyages remains undiminished, and that he has no plans to return to Kenya or Samoa until he has eaten his way through Spadina.
